What the worksheet does for you

  • Get unstuck without overthinking it. Answer a few plain questions from your own view, from a fair outsider's view, and from a year down the road.
  • Know when you've got your answer. Where all three readings agree, act. Where they don't, you'll know right where to dig.
  • Use it in 10 minutes, or 2. A full reading when you have time, a quick check when you don't.
  • Works on money, work, family, and big decisions. Print a stack and reach for one whenever you need it.

Three corners. One answer.

Surveyors have known it for a long time: one reading gives you a direction, two get you close, and three tell you where you are. The worksheet takes you to each corner in turn.

1

The Self

What do I feel, want, and fear here?

The inside view. On a service call, it's the customer's story. Here, the customer is you.

2

The Other

How does this look to the other people involved, or to a fair-minded stranger?

The outside view. It's what the gauges show: the facts from outside your own head.

3

The Future

How will this look a year from now? What would the wisest version of me do?

The long view. It's the experience that says what this usually turns out to be.

Where all three agree, that's your answer. Where they disagree, that's where the work is.

From a guy who's chased a lot of problems

I'm Michael Bartholomew. I've spent more than 25 years as an HVAC tech in Minnesota, crawling through attics and standing in mechanical rooms. The job taught me that you don't fix anything by staring harder at one part. You weigh what the customer tells you, what the equipment shows, and what experience says. When all three line up, you fix it with confidence.

I'm writing a book called Triangulate about using that same habit on the hard stuff in life. This worksheet is the whole method on one sheet of paper, front and back.
